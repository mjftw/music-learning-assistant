Pitch spiral — circle style
The practice screen's circle, unwound into a spiral: every note in range is a wedge, one ring per octave, lowest in the middle winding outward — so each note name sits on its own spoke with the circle's colours, and octaves read as distance from the hub. A needle across the band marks where you actually are (between wedges when you're off), with a short trail. One tap picks and closes; a C always shows its octave.

Target, when you want one
3a with a TARGET control. Left on auto it is a plain tuner — everything is measured from the nearest note. Pick a note (or tap the big name to hold what you're playing) and everything is measured from that instead: the big name stays on the target even in silence, the line pins to the edge and the tag counts semitones (▼ 2 st) when you're further than 50 ¢ off, and the stave shows the target as a grey head to the right of what you're playing. Try it with State → sweep or live.
4a
Auto or target
Tap TARGET for the sheet: Auto, Hold what you're playing, or tap a note in a chromatic strip covering the instrument's range — no keyboard, so it reads the same for any instrument (it opens scrolled to where you are; each C is edged darker; a dot marks the note you're playing). Once pinned, − / + nudge a semitone and ✕ returns to auto, without opening the sheet. The target is forgotten on leaving the tuner.

The stave shows where you are — notated properly
2c, with the heard note placed on the stave. One whole-note head, written as your instrument reads it, drifts at most ±3.5 px along a dotted guide at its true position — lifted when sharp, dropped when flat, never reaching the next staff position. The exact deviation is written in cents above it; the in-tune band sits behind.
3a
Written out
The note written as the instrument reads it, with the deviation in cents above it (as in Helmholtz–Ellis / contemporary notation). The head drifts at most ±3.5 px — never reaching the next staff position — along a dotted guide at its true place; a fading trail shows the last 2½ s. The clef is the instrument's and never changes (Tweaks → Instrument): flute treble, guitar treble-8 (written an octave up), cello bass. Notes outside the clef's register are written an octave in under 8va or 8vb, so the stave you read never swaps under you. The head is a whole-note shape, so no rhythm is implied.
